The step brief defines the record's sections and fields. This is how to do the analysis behind them
well, from the delivery lead's seat.

**Choose the technique from what the evidence looks like.** With rich data, the data-driven and
timeline techniques are the core. If the stage went badly for unclear reasons, add five-whys on its
two biggest costs. If it went well, add start-stop-continue so that good practice is captured and
not just problems. State the choice and the reason in the record. Follow the record's own section
structure (Frame, Diverge, Converge, Decisions, Non-decisions, Actions, KB write-back), and remember
that the diverge section lists what the data shows and the converge section explains it.

**Separate observation, cause and action.** For each finding write the observed fact with its number
and source, then a hypothesis about the cause, and only where the cause is credible an action.
Prefer causes in the process, the plan, the contracts or the tooling over causes in a role. Where a
finding rests on one data point, say so. Look especially at the gap between the sequencing plan and
what happened: which dependency slipped, which risk fired and whether it was on the register, which
estimate was wrong and by how much, and whether overlapping file claims serialised work that the
plan had assumed was parallel.

**Route each retained item to where it will be acted on**, using the routes the record gives you.
Process and ways-of-working changes go in the KB write-back section as exact entries to add or
change. A risk that changed status, or a new one, is stated as a decision or an action naming its
owner and the revised trigger, since you record it here rather than editing the risk register in
this step. A defect you noticed is described with its evidence and handed off to the role who
triages it. An item you considered and consciously did not take goes under non-decisions with a
revisit trigger.

**Keep the action list small enough to be done.** More than a handful of actions is a sign nothing
was prioritised. Each states what will change, the owning role, the point at which it is due, and
how the next retro will tell whether it worked. If an action from the previous retro was ignored
twice, say so and either escalate it or drop it openly.

**Note what you could not measure.** A measure you wanted and did not have (cost per story, rework,
flake rate) becomes an instrumentation action in its own right, because the next retro will need it.
Likewise, use the start, end and cost figures your context supplies for the record, and flag them as
unmeasured where it does not; never invent them.
